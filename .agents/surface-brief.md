# Daily Scheduler Dashboard

## Scope and mode
Operate-first schedule dashboard for a personal planning SaaS. The schedule is the primary product surface; supporting goal progress and lightweight recovery controls sit around it.

## Audience and job
A person with a demanding weekday routine needs to know what to do today, why it matters, and how the plan adapts when the day changes. The critical action is completing or skipping a scheduled task with low friction.

## Direction contract

### THESIS
Daily Scheduler should feel like a humane planning desk, not a chatbot or a generic calendar. It refuses the category-default arrangement of charts and empty productivity cards by opening on today's actual work, with the goal and recovery logic visible beside it.

### OWN-WORLD
A pale mineral canvas, ink-black type, moss green for completed work, and ember orange for attention. Thin rules, restrained rounded surfaces, compact timeline rows, and a single warm status marker create the visual language of a well-used field notebook translated into a modern operating tool.

### STORY
The user immediately understands that their plan is alive but bounded: today's work is clear, fixed commitments are respected, and the system has already made room for progress. The visitor completes a task, creates a goal, or opens the week view.

### FIRST VIEWPORT
A slim left rail anchors identity and navigation. The main column begins with a plain-language date and a large “Good morning, Maya” heading, followed by the goal progress strip and today's schedule as the dominant element. A narrow right column holds the week pulse, constraints, and a small replanning prompt. The primary task action lives directly in the first schedule row.

### FORM
A schedule-led operate surface, derived from the field notebook and daily dispatch traditions rather than a dashboard template. The signature interaction is “shift the plan” from a missed task: a skip reason becomes a visible redistribution suggestion without disturbing work or sleep.
